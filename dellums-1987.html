Ronald V. Dellums

Photographs associated with Congressman Ronald V. Dellums, whose public career in Berkeley and Oakland was marked by advocacy for civil rights, opposition to the Vietnam War, and efforts against apartheid in South Africa.

The public figure

Public service in the East Bay

Ronald V. Dellums began his elected political career on the Berkeley City Council in 1967. After winning election to Congress in 1970, he took office in January 1971, representing a district that included Oakland and Berkeley. He became known nationally for his opposition to the Vietnam War and military spending and for his advocacy on civil rights and against apartheid in South Africa. For further context, see the U.S. House of Representatives’ historical biography.

The photographs

Documented occasions

May 31, 1987 · Oakland

Twenty Years of Public Service

On May 31, 1987, guests gathered at Oakland’s Hyatt Regency Hotel to honor Dellums’s twenty years in elected office. The evening featured speeches, music, and the presentation of a commemorative plaque.

The original invitation documents the planned date, venue, and program. The published gallery contains 41 photographs and three accompanying printed objects. The photographs are attributed to Jacob Blanchette by the collection steward; individual exposure dates and many identifications remain under research. These are preliminary non-generative reproductions, and reproduction rights remain under review. The creators of the printed objects are not established.
