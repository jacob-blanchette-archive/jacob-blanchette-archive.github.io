The photographer

Jacob Blanchette

A biography of the Oakland photographer, illustrated with photographs from his life and work.

Research in progress. Some details in this biography are based on family testimony and remain subject to further documentary verification.

Early Life and Family

Jacob Samuel Blanchette Jr. was born on October 18, 1931, in Beaumont, Texas, to Agnes and Jacob S. Blanchette Sr. His family's history in Beaumont was closely connected to the city's segregated educational institutions. In 1909, his great-grandfather, Ozan Blanchette, and Usan Hebert, both formerly enslaved men, donated land for a school serving Black children in the Pear Orchard neighborhood. Originally known as South Park Colored School, it later became Hebert High School. His father was among the school's first five graduates in 1924 and later taught there.

Around 1941, Jacob moved with his mother to Oakland, California. His sister, Esther, joined them in California later. Their move took place during the Second Great Migration, as growing numbers of African Americans left the South for cities in the North and West. Jacob grew up in West Oakland during a period of considerable change. The expansion of wartime industries, particularly shipbuilding, brought thousands of Black migrants to the Bay Area. Although these industries provided new employment opportunities, racial discrimination continued to shape housing and employment in Oakland.

Education and Military Service

Jacob attended Berkeley High School in the late 1940s. From 1952 to 1956, he served in the United States Air Force, attaining the rank of Airman Second Class. His service took place during the desegregation of the American armed forces, following President Harry S. Truman's 1948 executive order establishing a policy of equal treatment and opportunity regardless of race.

His professional work

Photographic Career

Following his military service, Jacob studied photography at the Joseph C. Laney Trade and Technical Institute in Oakland, then part of Oakland Junior College. He later established his own business, Jacob Blanchette Photographic Services, working across portraiture, public ceremonies, and cultural events.

His photographic career also included assignments for the federal government. He received an Army Certificate of Achievement recognizing his work as a photographer for the Western Area of the Military Traffic Management and Terminal Service, including special assignments supporting its commanding general. On March 27, 1987, he retired from Naval Supply Center Oakland after 35 years of federal government service.

Oakland's Black film community

During the 1980s, Jacob photographed events associated with Oakland's Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame. Founded in 1974, the organization recognized the contributions of Black filmmakers and performers through its annual Oscar Micheaux Awards. His photographs documented awards presentations and gatherings associated with these events.

Oakland's Black theater community

In 1990, Jacob photographed the Black Repertory Group's 25th anniversary celebration. Founded in Oakland in 1965, the theater organization presented productions and provided opportunities for Black performers. His photographs of the anniversary celebration include speakers, presentations, group portraits, and informal gatherings.
