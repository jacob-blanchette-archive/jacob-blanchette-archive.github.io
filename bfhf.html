A landmark cultural institution

The Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame was established in Oakland in the 1970s to recognize the contributions of Black artists and professionals to cinema. Its activities included awards ceremonies and other events celebrating Black film history and achievement.

Jacob Blanchette Jr.'s photographs offer a perspective on the people, gatherings, and cultural history associated with these events. The developing gallery brings together 35 digitized objects from one binder, including a printed announcement for the February 22, 1987 Oscar Micheaux Awards ceremony in Oakland, photographs of presentations and receptions, and several mounted film/contact assemblies. The announcement provides a historical point of reference, but does not establish the date or event depicted in each photograph.

This is a working public gallery of first-pass digitizations. The original binder sequence is provisionally recorded; some crops and reflections require review. People, dates, individual photographer credits and reproduction rights are under investigation. Photographs may document more than one event.

Historical sources

Relevant institutional collections include the Oakland Public Library's Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame collection and Indiana University's Black Film Center & Archive. These support historical context but do not independently identify subjects in Jacob's images.
