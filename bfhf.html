The organization

Recognition, film, and cultural history

The Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame began in Oakland in 1974 as a project of the Oakland Museum Association’s Cultural and Ethnic Affairs Guild. Mary Perry Smith and Margot Smith Hicks were among its founders. The organization developed the annual Oscar Micheaux Awards to recognize Black filmmakers and performers, including artists whose contributions had often gone unacknowledged in mainstream film histories.

The awards were presented at Oakland’s Paramount Theatre from 1974 to 1993. The Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame also supported film screenings, educational programs, independent filmmakers, and competitions. Its activities extended beyond the awards ceremony itself, drawing participants to symposia, performances, and social gatherings across the Bay Area.

Jacob Blanchette photographed events associated with the organization over several years. His photographs show people receiving honors, making presentations, performing onstage, and gathering around these occasions.

Events and photographs

The 1987 programs

Materials preserved with the first 1987 binder announce three distinct occasions: a February 21 film symposium at Wheeler Auditorium, University of California, Berkeley; a gala dinner and dance that evening at The Concourse in San Francisco; and the Fourteenth Annual Oscar Micheaux Awards on February 22 at Oakland’s Paramount Theatre. The associated photographs include portraits, a panel discussion, and handprint or signing activity, although their individual event assignments have not been established.

The second 1987 binder includes photographs of presentations, groups onstage, and related film/contact material. A reference to a Black film festival in Chicago in June 1987 raises a separate historical question. The dated programs are evidence for advertised occasions, not proof that every print was exposed at a particular event or venue.

The 1988 photographs

The 1988 binders extend the record through award presentations, musicians and singers performing, stage scenes, and formal portraits of attendees. One includes a printed program for the Fifteenth Annual Oscar Micheaux Awards. The photographs and program belong to the same archival grouping, but their presence together does not independently establish the date or setting of every image.

Research context

Historical Sources

The Oakland Public Library’s Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame collection documents the organization’s founding and award programs. Indiana University’s Black Film Center & Archive holds the Mary Perry Smith archives and further photographic and event records. These repositories provide historical context for the material photographed by Jacob Blanchette; they do not by themselves identify the people or specific occasions in each print.
