The organization

Recognition, film, and cultural history

Founded in Oakland in 1974 as a project of the Oakland Museum Association’s Cultural and Ethnic Affairs Guild, the Black Filmmakers Hall of Fame developed the Oscar Micheaux Awards to honor Black filmmakers and performers whose achievements had often been overlooked in mainstream film histories. Mary Perry Smith and Margot Smith Hicks were among its founders.

The annual awards were presented at Oakland’s Paramount Theatre from 1974 to 1993. Screenings, film lectures, competitions, and symposia extended the Hall of Fame’s work beyond the ceremony, bringing filmmakers, performers, and audiences together across the Bay Area.

Jacob Blanchette photographed presentations, musical performances, portraits, and gatherings associated with the Hall of Fame over several years. His photographs show both the public stage and the occasions surrounding the organization’s programs.

Historical background: Oakland Public Library and Indiana University’s Black Film Center & Archive.
